Nebraska Senate race

Pete Ricketts holds the seat and is running again. Candidates: the Democratic and Republican nominees and the independent or third-party candidates polling 5% or more or recognised as the main opposition, incumbent first.

Pete Ricketts

Republican Incumbent73% on Kalshi

U.S. senator, running for re-election · Campaign website

On the record

  • IsraelVoted

    On April 15, 2026, Ricketts voted no on a motion to discharge S.J.Res. 32, a joint resolution of disapproval that would have blocked a proposed U.S. military sale of defense articles and services to the Government of Israel. The motion failed 40-59.

    Roll call: Roll Call Vote 119th Congress - 2nd Session, Vote Number 80, U.S. Senate, April 15, 2026

  • ICESaid

    In a June 5, 2026 statement after the Senate passed a bill funding U.S. Border Patrol and ICE, Ricketts said he voted against amendments to the bill and for its final passage, and that the officers deserve support.

    "I voted for final passage of the initial legislation, which will fund our Border Patrol and ICE."

    Ricketts Issues Statement After Bill to Fund Border Patrol and ICE Passes Senate, U.S. Senate (office of Sen. Pete Ricketts), June 5, 2026

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    In a Jan. 14, 2026 statement and video marking the first year of President Trump's second term, Ricketts said the administration and Senate Republicans had made streets safer, cut taxes and restored "peace through strength," and said he is working with the president.

    "After a year, President Trump and Senate Republicans are getting America back on track."

    Ricketts Highlights Successes of President Trump’s First Year Back in Office, U.S. Senate (office of Sen. Pete Ricketts), January 14, 2026

  • Where they want the country to goSaid

    Asked in the Nebraska Examiner's 2026 voter guide (primary-season question) what Congress's role should be toward the president, Ricketts said that under the Trump administration he is working alongside the president, and listed his votes for military funding, border enforcement funding and the 2025 tax cuts.

    "Under the Trump administration, I’m working alongside the President to keep Americans safe."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, April 17, 2026

  • The economySaid

    In his answer to the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide, Ricketts cited his votes for the 2025 tax law (no tax on tips and overtime, the Social Security income tax change, a larger child tax credit) and for the ROAD to Housing Act as steps on affordability.

    "Cost of living will stay my top priority in the Senate."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

  • Foreign policySaid

    At a June 8, 2026 tele-town hall, asked about the U.S. war with Iran and its cost to farmers, Ricketts said the goal is to stop Iran from building a nuclear weapon and to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and that negotiating a nuclear deal could take several months.

    "The crux of what we’re trying to accomplish is to get Iran to give up making a nuclear bomb."

    Nebraska Sen. Pete Ricketts fields questions on Iran, women’s sports during tele-town hall, Nebraska Examiner, June 8, 2026

  • ImmigrationSaid

    Asked in the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide about the Supreme Court ruling against President Trump's birthright citizenship order, Ricketts said the issue requires congressional action and that he co-sponsors a bill to change citizenship at birth for some children of non-citizens.

    "I'm a co-sponsor of the Birthright Citizenship Act to end automatic citizenship for children of temporary visa holders and illegal immigrants."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

  • Democracy and electionsSaid

    Asked in the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide what election changes he supports, Ricketts pointed to his votes for the SAVE Act, which would require proof of citizenship and voter ID nationwide, and said he will keep pushing for it.

    "I’ve voted for the SAVE Act five times in the Senate to bring commonsense voter ID laws nationwide."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

  • Money in politicsSaid

    In a July 22, 2026 op-ed, Ricketts promoted his Stop Insider Trading Act, which would bar members of Congress and their families from buying new individual stocks and require public notice before sales, and argued against forcing lawmakers to divest.

    "To be clear, no lawmaker should profit from insider information. My Stop Insider Trading Act is the solution."

    Ricketts Op-Ed: It’s Time to Ban Congressional Insider Trading, U.S. Senate (office of Sen. Pete Ricketts), July 22, 2026

Dan Osborn

Independent 27% on Kalshi

Campaign website

On the record

  • IsraelSaid

    Asked in the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide how Congress should handle international conflicts, Osborn said he supports both the Israeli and Palestinian peoples but not the Israeli government's current actions, and that the U.S. should not fund offensive wars or weapons used on schools and hospitals.

    "On Israel, I support the Israeli people and their right to exist, and I support the Palestinian people as well. But I don't support what the Israeli government is currently doing."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

  • ICESaid

    In the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide, Osborn said he agrees with the basic mission of keeping communities safe and credited Trump on border security, but called the increase in ICE's budget wasteful and called for comprehensive immigration reform including mandatory E-Verify and guest worker programs.

    "However, the massive increase in ICE's budget has been wasteful spending without real accountability."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    In a statement quoted by the Nebraska Examiner on Sept. 24, 2026, issued after a leaked recording of his remarks on the border wall, Osborn said he supports strong border security, gives President Trump credit for securing the border, and would support a wall "where it makes sense."

    "When I give President Trump full credit for securing the border, some people don’t like that. But because I’m an independent, I don’t have to blindly support the purity tests of either party."

    Nebraska Senate race has seen tough talk and shifts on Immigration, Nebraska Examiner, September 24, 2026

  • Where they want the country to goSaid

    Asked in the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide what Congress's role should be toward the president, Osborn said Congress has failed to check presidents of both parties and that governing by executive action and reconciliation cannot continue.

    "Congress is supposed to be the check on the executive branch, not a rubber stamp for whoever's in the White House, and it's failed at that job under both recent presidents."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

  • Foreign policySaid

    Speaking at an endorsement event in Papillion, reported Oct. 13, 2026, Osborn criticized the U.S. war with Iran and called for ending it, saying it has raised gas, diesel and grocery prices.

    "The people I talked to, their lives are exponentially harder because of this war"

    Cost of living looms over Nebraska Senate race, Nebraska Examiner, October 13, 2026

  • Social Security and MedicareSaid

    In the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide, Osborn proposed applying the Social Security payroll tax to income above the current cap of $176,100, and said he would look first to the Pentagon and defense contractors for spending cuts.

    "Closing that gap alone would extend Social Security's solvency for decades without cutting a single benefit."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

  • Money in politicsSaid

    In the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide, Osborn said he supports overturning Citizens United, the DISCLOSE Act, independent redistricting commissions, and term and age limits for Congress.

    "I support the DISCLOSE Act, which would require any group spending more than $10,000 to influence an election to say where that money came from."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

  • Health careSaid

    Asked in the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide which legislation he would champion on affordability, Osborn named the Break Up Big Medicine Act, which targets companies that own insurers, providers and pharmacies at once.

    "The Break Up Big Medicine Act would stop one company from owning your insurance, your doctor and your pharmacy all at once"

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

  • ImmigrationSaid

    Asked in the Nebraska Examiner's general-election voter guide about the Supreme Court ruling against President Trump's birthright citizenship order, Osborn said he agrees with the ruling and does not support changing birthright citizenship.

    "Six justices, including Trump appointees, agreed that the Constitution has said the same thing since 1868: if you're born here, you're a citizen."

    U.S. Senate | Voter Guide: 2026 General Election, Nebraska Examiner, September 17, 2026

Your script

Hi, my name is ____, and I'm a constituent from ____, ZIP ____.

I'm calling about AIPAC money, ICE and checks and balances.

I want my senator to refuse money from AIPAC PAC and United Democracy Project, and to say so publicly.

I want my senator to vote against more money for ICE detention and enforcement.

I want my senator to use Congress's oversight power, including hearings, subpoenas and votes, whichever party is in charge.

Please record my position. Thank you for your time.

Some offices log one issue per call. If they do, call back for the rest.

Call

    Tips for calling
    • Call your own senators and representative. Offices mostly count calls from their own voters.
    • Say your ZIP so the call counts as a constituent.
    • You'll talk to a staff member, not the official. That's normal: they log your call.
    • One or two issues per call is best.
    • Voicemail counts. Offices go through it every day.
    • If the D.C. office is busy, try a state office, or the U.S. Capitol Switchboard at (202) 224-3121.
    • Call again next week.
