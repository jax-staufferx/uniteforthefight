On the record

    No public statement found as of October 18, 2026.

    No public statement found as of October 18, 2026.

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    At a campaign stop in Hendersonville on August 20, 2026, he criticized the President for saying there was money for the Iran war but not enough for child care. The Hendersonville Lightning reported the remark and his campaign reposted it.

    "This president, reinforced by Michael Whatley, my opponent, has said we’ve got to spend money on the Iran War and that we don’t have enough to spend on childcare, which is so wrong,"

    ICYMI: Roy Cooper Continues “Make Stuff Cost Less” Tour in Hendersonville, Candidate's campaign site (quoting the Hendersonville Lightning), August 20, 2026

  • Where they want the country to goSaid

    At a Greensboro rally on October 14, 2026, he said lowering costs starts with working with Congress to roll back the current tariffs and find a way to end the war in Iran. WFMY and the Greensboro News & Record reported it, and his campaign reposted it.

    "It starts with working with Congress to roll back these chaotic tariffs that we know are adding to the cost of everyday families. It means finding a way to end this war in Iran."

    ICYMI: Roy Cooper Continues “Make Stuff Cost Less” Tour in Greensboro, Candidate's campaign site (quoting WFMY), October 14, 2026

  • Foreign policySaid

    In a campaign statement for a September 10, 2026 event in Goldsboro, he said the war with Iran had cost the lives of American service members and raised prices for North Carolinians.

    "American service members have died and prices have gone through the roof for working people here in Wayne County and all across the state because of this reckless war with Iran"

    Roy Cooper Continues “Make Stuff Cost Less” Tour in Goldsboro, Candidate's campaign site, September 10, 2026

  • Trade and tariffsSaid

    His campaign's grocery-cost plan says the administration's tariffs are raising farmers' costs and food prices, and that he would end them.

    "Roy would put an end to these tariffs, and make sure that farmers who keep our state moving can thrive."

    Lowering the Cost of Food & Groceries | Roy Cooper for North Carolina, Candidate's campaign site, September 5, 2026

  • Health careSaid

    His campaign's health care plan calls for reinstating the expired Affordable Care Act tax credits and reversing Medicaid cuts. It also calls for broader Medicare drug-price negotiation and a cap on yearly out-of-pocket prescription costs.

    "Reinstate the ACA tax credits and reverse Medicaid cuts."

    Making Health Care Affordable | Roy Cooper for North Carolina, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

  • EnergySaid

    His campaign's energy plan says data centers should pay the full cost of the power they use instead of passing it to consumers. It also calls for banning utility shutoffs during extreme heat and cold.

    "Make sure data centers pay the full cost of all the power they use and prevent costs from being passed on to consumers."

    Lowering Energy & Utility Costs | Roy Cooper for North Carolina, Candidate's campaign site, September 5, 2026

  • Money in politicsSaid

    His campaign says he does not own or trade individual stocks and supports a ban on stock trading by members of Congress. It also says he has committed not to become a lobbyist after leaving office.

    "supports a ban on congressional stock trading and has committed to not becoming a lobbyist after leaving office"

    NEW From WRAL: Roy Cooper Supports A Congressional Stock Ban. Michael Whatley Supports Policies That Boost His Stocks., Candidate's campaign site, August 7, 2026

On the record

  • Israel

    No public statement found as of October 18, 2026.

  • ICESaid

    His campaign's issues page says he fully supports President Trump securing the border and deporting people in the country illegally who have committed crimes. It also criticizes Cooper's veto, as governor, of a bill requiring sheriffs to cooperate with ICE.

    "I am 100% supportive of President Trump securing the border and deporting the millions of criminal illegal aliens that Joe Biden and Roy Cooper allowed into this country."

    Issues – Michael Whatley for Senate, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    In prepared remarks to the RNC Midterm Convention on September 9, 2026, he credited President Trump's leadership for the 2025 tax law, which Republicans call the Working Families Tax Cuts.

    "Under President Trump’s leadership, Republicans passed the Working Families Tax Cuts… and put thousands of dollars back into the hands of families and our seniors."

    Michael Whatley Slams Roy Cooper’s Soft-on-Crime Agenda and Record of Raising Costs at Republican Midterm Convention, Candidate's campaign site, September 9, 2026

  • Where they want the country to goSaid

    His campaign's issues page says he would advance President Trump's America First agenda as a senator. It also says Republicans must hold the seat to keep their Senate majority.

    "I am committed to advancing President Trump’s America First agenda as the next U.S. Senator from North Carolina."

    Issues – Michael Whatley for Senate, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

  • TaxesSaid

    In a July 4, 2026 statement marking a year since the 2025 tax law was signed, he praised it and its tax breaks on tips, overtime and Social Security. He said he would keep working to enact similar policies.

    "I will always work to enact policies like the Working Families Tax Cuts which allow you to make more and keep more of your money"

    Whatley On The One Year Anniversary of the Working Families Tax Cuts Being Signed Into Law, Candidate's campaign site, July 4, 2026

  • Health careSaid

    In the same September 9, 2026 convention remarks, he credited the TrumpRx program and most-favored-nation drug pricing with lowering prescription drug costs.

    "Through TrumpRX and reinstating Most Favored Nation Status, Republicans have helped Americans to get their prescription drugs at the lowest possible cost."

    Michael Whatley Slams Roy Cooper’s Soft-on-Crime Agenda and Record of Raising Costs at Republican Midterm Convention, Candidate's campaign site, September 9, 2026

  • Crime and policingSaid

    Accepting the North Carolina Fraternal Order of Police endorsement on August 12, 2026, he said he would work with law enforcement on public safety. His campaign also criticizes Cooper's record on bail and prisoner releases as governor.

    "As your next Senator, I will work with our dedicated law enforcement officers to keep our kids and communities safe"

    North Carolina Fraternal Order of Police Endorses Michael Whatley for US Senate, Candidate's campaign site, August 12, 2026

  • EnergySaid

    His campaign's issues page says expanding American energy production and limiting federal spending would create jobs and achieve energy independence.

    "By practicing fiscal responsibility and unleashing American energy, we can empower American workers, create good-paying jobs, achieve energy independence, and build lasting prosperity for every American."

    Issues – Michael Whatley for Senate, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

Your script

Hi, my name is ____, and I'm a constituent from ____, ZIP ____.

I'm calling about AIPAC money, ICE and checks and balances.

I want my senator to refuse money from AIPAC PAC and United Democracy Project, and to say so publicly.

I want my senator to vote against more money for ICE detention and enforcement.

Some offices log one issue per call. If they do, call back for the rest.

Call

    Tips for calling
    • Call your own senators and representative. Offices mostly count calls from their own voters.
    • Say your ZIP so the call counts as a constituent.
    • You'll talk to a staff member, not the official. That's normal: they log your call.
    • One or two issues per call is best.
    • Voicemail counts. Offices go through it every day.
    • If the D.C. office is busy, try a state office, or the U.S. Capitol Switchboard at (202) 224-3121.
    • Call again next week.
