New Mexico Senate race

Ben Ray Luján holds the seat and is running again. Candidates: the Democratic and Republican nominees, incumbent first.

Ben Ray Luján

Democratic Incumbent97% on Kalshi

U.S. senator, running for re-election · Campaign website

On the record

  • IsraelVoted

    On April 15, 2026, he voted yes on a motion to discharge S.J.Res. 32, a resolution disapproving a proposed sale of US weapons and defense services to the Government of Israel. The motion failed 40-59.

    Roll call: U.S. Senate: U.S. Senate Roll Call Votes 119th Congress - 2nd Session, U.S. Senate, April 15, 2026

  • ICEVoted

    On June 4, 2026, he voted yes on a motion to waive budget rules for Senate Amendment 5485 to S. 2, which would have moved funding from US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) to the Child Care and Development Block Grant program. The motion failed 46-53.

    Roll call: U.S. Senate: U.S. Senate Roll Call Votes 119th Congress - 2nd Session, U.S. Senate, June 4, 2026

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    When he filed for re-election on February 3, 2026, he said the Trump administration had undermined jobs in New Mexico, put rural hospitals at risk of closing and raised costs for working families.

    "Instead of building on the progress made, the Trump administration has undermined jobs here at home, put rural hospitals on the brink of closure, and driven up costs for working families."

    Ben Ray Luján Files for Re-Election to the U.S. Senate: “New Mexico is a special place that we are proud to call home.”, Candidate's campaign site, February 3, 2026

  • Where they want the country to goSaid

    In his statement after winning the Democratic primary on June 2, 2026, he said he would keep opposing the Trump administration's agenda in the Senate.

    "I will continue standing up to the Trump administration’s cruel agenda and working to make sure federal investments reach every corner of our state."

    NEWS – Luján Earns Democratic Senate Nomination, Vows to Keep Fighting for New Mexico, Candidate's campaign site, June 2, 2026

  • Foreign policySaid

    In an Albuquerque Journal candidate questionnaire published October 2, 2026, he said he has opposed the US war with Iran from the start and called for an immediate end to it.

    "I’ve opposed President Trump’s war since day one."

    U.S. Senate: Ben Ray Lújan (D), Albuquerque Journal, October 2, 2026

  • Trade and tariffsSaid

    In the same Albuquerque Journal questionnaire, he said tariffs can help when used strategically, but that the Trump administration's broad tariffs have raised prices for New Mexico families.

    "But the sweeping tariffs put in place by the Trump administration have raised the price of everything."

    U.S. Senate: Ben Ray Lújan (D), Albuquerque Journal, October 2, 2026

  • Democracy and electionsSaid

    Asked in the Albuquerque Journal questionnaire about requiring voter ID and proof of citizenship, he said he is open to such protections. He said he rejected the most recent Republican voter ID bill.

    "I rejected the most recent Republican voter ID bill because it would threaten voter privacy and the secret ballot, disrupt mail and absentee voting, and impose requirements more restrictive than nearly every state’s existing laws."

    U.S. Senate: Ben Ray Lújan (D), Albuquerque Journal, October 2, 2026

  • TaxesVoted

    On July 1, 2025, he voted no on final Senate passage of H.R. 1, the budget reconciliation bill that extended the 2017 tax cuts and changed Medicaid and food assistance. The vote was tied 50-50 and the Vice President broke the tie, so the bill passed.

    Roll call: U.S. Senate: U.S. Senate Roll Call Votes 119th Congress - 1st Session, U.S. Senate, July 1, 2025

  • TechnologySaid

    In the Albuquerque Journal questionnaire, he called the risk of fast AI growth existential. He said he has introduced legislation to set guardrails and believes states should be able to enforce their own protections.

    "I’ve introduced legislation to establish guardrails, support a select committee on AI, and believe states should be able to enforce protections."

    U.S. Senate: Ben Ray Lújan (D), Albuquerque Journal, October 2, 2026

On the record

  • Israel

    No public statement found as of October 18, 2026.

  • ICESaid

    His campaign's border security page says state policies that limit cooperation with federal immigration authorities make it harder to remove dangerous offenders. It also says he would vote to block nominees who do not publicly support enforcing existing immigration laws.

    "State policies that block meaningful cooperation with federal immigration authorities make it harder to remove dangerous offenders and easier for criminal networks to keep operating."

    Secure New Mexico Borders - Larry "Lead" Marker, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    In an October 2026 Albuquerque Journal interview, the paper reported that he disagrees with some of President Trump's policies. He objected to Trump's social media calls to rename New Mexico "New America".

    "He needs to shut the hell up about the New America thing."

    US Sen. Ben Ray Luján faces long-shot challenge from Republican Larry Marker in reelection bid, Albuquerque Journal, October 8, 2026

  • Where they want the country to go

    No public statement found as of October 18, 2026.

  • Foreign policySaid

    In an Albuquerque Journal candidate questionnaire published October 2, 2026, he said he supports decisive action against Iran's nuclear ambitions. He said the war should end with a clear military and diplomatic resolution, not an open-ended commitment.

    "I support decisive action to neutralize the Iranians regime nuclear ambitions and protect American and allied lives."

    U.S. Senate: Larry Marker (R), Albuquerque Journal, October 2, 2026

  • Trade and tariffsSaid

    In the same questionnaire, he said he supports using tariffs strategically as leverage against countries such as China, rather than as a blanket tax on American consumers.

    "I support using tariffs strategically to protect American workers, farmers and industries from unfair foreign competition."

    U.S. Senate: Larry Marker (R), Albuquerque Journal, October 2, 2026

  • Democracy and electionsSaid

    Asked in the Albuquerque Journal questionnaire about federal legislation requiring voter ID and proof of citizenship to register and vote, he said he strongly supports both.

    "I strongly support requiring voter ID and proof of citizenship."

    U.S. Senate: Larry Marker (R), Albuquerque Journal, October 2, 2026

  • EnergySaid

    His campaign's energy page pledges legislation setting deadlines for federal permits to drill on federal land. A permit would be deemed approved if an agency misses the deadline.

    "I will codify into law a requirement that federal agencies must process applications for permits to drill on federal lands within a set timeframe."

    New Mexico Energy Independence - Larry "Lead" Marker, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

  • FarmingSaid

    His campaign's agriculture page calls for stopping what it calls regulatory abuse of farmers and ranchers. It calls for protecting grazing rights and water access, and requiring federal agencies to decide on a set timetable.

    "Ranchers and farmers should not need a legal defense team just to keep doing honest work on the land."

    Agriculture & Grazing Rights - Larry "Lead" Marker, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

Your script

Hi, my name is ____, and I'm a constituent from ____, ZIP ____.

I'm calling about AIPAC money, ICE and checks and balances.

I want my senator to refuse money from AIPAC PAC and United Democracy Project, and to say so publicly.

I want my senator to vote against more money for ICE detention and enforcement.

I want my senator to use Congress's oversight power, including hearings, subpoenas and votes, whichever party is in charge.

Please record my position. Thank you for your time.

Some offices log one issue per call. If they do, call back for the rest.

Call

    Tips for calling
    • Call your own senators and representative. Offices mostly count calls from their own voters.
    • Say your ZIP so the call counts as a constituent.
    • You'll talk to a staff member, not the official. That's normal: they log your call.
    • One or two issues per call is best.
    • Voicemail counts. Offices go through it every day.
    • If the D.C. office is busy, try a state office, or the U.S. Capitol Switchboard at (202) 224-3121.
    • Call again next week.
