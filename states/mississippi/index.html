Mississippi Senate race

Cindy Hyde-Smith holds the seat and is running again. Candidates: the Democratic and Republican nominees and the independent or third-party candidates polling 5% or more or recognised as the main opposition, incumbent first.

Cindy Hyde-Smith

Republican Incumbent88% on Kalshi

U.S. senator, running for re-election · Campaign website

On the record

  • IsraelVoted

    On April 15, 2026, Hyde-Smith voted Nay on a motion to discharge S.J.Res. 32, a resolution of disapproval of a proposed U.S. foreign military sale of defense articles and services to the Government of Israel. The motion was rejected 40-59.

    Roll call: U.S. Senate Roll Call Votes 119th Congress - 2nd Session, Vote Number 80, U.S. Senate, April 15, 2026

  • ICESaid

    On August 6, 2026, Hyde-Smith joined Sen. Katie Britt in reintroducing the Keep Our Communities Safe Act, which would expand the Department of Homeland Security's authority to keep immigrants with criminal records in detention beyond the six-month limit set by the Supreme Court's 2001 Zadvydas v. Davis ruling.

    "closing this 'catch and release' loophole will ultimately keep dangerous illegals detained instead of being freed to put law-abiding Americans, law enforcement, and our communities at risk"

    HYDE-SMITH RENEWS SUPPORT TO CLOSE LEGAL LOOPHOLE REQUIRING RELEASE OF ALIENS FROM IMMIGRATION DETENTION, U.S. Senate (office of Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith), August 6, 2026

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    In a July 2026 interview with Magnolia Tribune, Hyde-Smith said President Trump's help had benefited Mississippi, citing a rural hospital in Franklin County, and spoke positively about what he has done.

    "my goodness, he's done so many great things that are so positive that hard working people like the people in Mississippi are so grateful for and they recognize it, they appreciate it, and they like us being a team"

    With President Trump in her corner, Senator Hyde-Smith doesn't pull punches as she seeks re-election, Magnolia Tribune, July 21, 2026

  • Where they want the country to goVoted

    On July 1, 2025, Hyde-Smith voted Yea on Senate passage of H.R. 1, the reconciliation bill known as the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which carried President Trump's tax, spending and immigration-enforcement agenda. The Senate vote was 50-50 and the Vice President broke the tie.

    Roll call: U.S. Senate Roll Call Votes 119th Congress - 1st Session, Vote Number 372, U.S. Senate, July 1, 2025

  • Foreign policySaid

    Asked in July 2026 about the conflict with Iran, Hyde-Smith said she supports the President on it, that Iran must not have nuclear weapons, and that the effects on consumers are a price worth paying for security.

    "obviously Iran does not need to have nuclear weapons and there's a price to pay to get to where we need to be to have a secure country"

    With President Trump in her corner, Senator Hyde-Smith doesn't pull punches as she seeks re-election, Magnolia Tribune, July 21, 2026

  • ImmigrationSaid

    In the same July 2026 interview, Hyde-Smith said immigration reform is needed, that agriculture employers need H-2A and H-2B guest workers, and that people in the country who are deportable should be deported.

    "We've got to have workers. We've got to know who you are. We've got to know where you are. And if you're deportable, you're just deportable."

    With President Trump in her corner, Senator Hyde-Smith doesn't pull punches as she seeks re-election, Magnolia Tribune, July 21, 2026

  • Democracy and electionsSaid

    Hyde-Smith is a cosponsor of the SAVE America Act, which would set national voter ID standards and is meant to keep noncitizens from voting. In July 2026 she said she fully supports it.

    "So, I'm 100% for the SAVE Act to try to get that past."

    With President Trump in her corner, Senator Hyde-Smith doesn't pull punches as she seeks re-election, Magnolia Tribune, July 21, 2026

  • AbortionSaid

    On June 24, 2026, the fourth anniversary of the Dobbs decision, Hyde-Smith, who chairs the Senate Pro-Life Caucus, joined Sen. Mike Rounds in introducing the Dismemberment Abortion Ban Act of 2026, a federal ban on dilation and evacuation abortion procedures with criminal penalties for physicians.

    "Every pre-born child is precious, and it's appalling that any physician trained to save lives would dismember a child in the womb."

    HYDE-SMITH, ROUNDS INTRODUCE BILL BANNING BARBARIC ABORTION PROCEDURES, U.S. Senate (office of Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith), June 24, 2026

  • Health careSaid

    In July 2026, Hyde-Smith said lowering prescription drug prices is a priority for her and called pharmacy benefit managers "the biggest racket in the entire world."

    "if we could get that one thing done for pharmaceutical drugs to bring the price down on that to where they should be and to take out the absolute unbelievable gouging of the American people. I am so committed to that."

    With President Trump in her corner, Senator Hyde-Smith doesn't pull punches as she seeks re-election, Magnolia Tribune, July 21, 2026

On the record

    No public statement found as of October 18, 2026.

    In a July 2026 interview, Colom said he supports a "strong, secure border" and that immigration enforcement must also respect due process, citing his work as a district attorney.

    "As a District Attorney, I can tell you how important it is that every citizen or everybody has the same, you know, due process that the constitution guarantees, so that's my position on it"

    Mississippi hasn't elected a Democrat to the U.S. Senate since 1982. Scott Colom believes he will change that in November, Magnolia Tribune, July 17, 2026

    In a July 2026 interview, Colom criticized President Trump's cryptocurrency earnings, calling them corruption, and criticized Hyde-Smith's votes in connection with it.

    "I mean, if you look at the crypto money that our president made, $1.4 billion in crypto in one year, you look at our junior Senator voting to allow that type of corruption to happen"

    Mississippi hasn't elected a Democrat to the U.S. Senate since 1982. Scott Colom believes he will change that in November, Magnolia Tribune, July 17, 2026

    In his March 10, 2026 primary-night speech, Colom said "we are headed in the wrong direction," citing what Mississippians told him about tariffs, prices and low wages, blamed Hyde-Smith's leadership, and called for "a new direction."

    "we are headed in the wrong direction, y'all. We're heading in the wrong direction."

  • Trade and tariffsSaid

    In July 2026, Colom said tariffs are a tax and that Congress should get its voice back on tariffs, and criticized Hyde-Smith for supporting the President's tariffs.

    "The conservative position is low tariffs. Our junior Senator used to be a free trader. She only became a protectionist because the person in D.C. changed."

    Mississippi hasn't elected a Democrat to the U.S. Senate since 1982. Scott Colom believes he will change that in November, Magnolia Tribune, July 17, 2026

  • Health careSaid

    In July 2026, Colom said Mississippi should expand Medicaid and that he wants to roll back the health care cuts in the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

    "It's a good deal for Mississippi. It gives people health insurance. It helps our hospitals get the money they need because the alternative is uncompensated care, because people don't stop getting sick"

    Mississippi hasn't elected a Democrat to the U.S. Senate since 1982. Scott Colom believes he will change that in November, Magnolia Tribune, July 17, 2026

  • GunsSaid

    Speaking at the Neshoba County Fair on June 24, 2026, Colom said he would not compromise Second Amendment rights.

    "That means I won't compromise the Second Amendment rights that allow me to keep my daughter's safe and allows you to keep your family safe."

    Congressional candidates stump at Neshoba, Magnolia Tribune, June 24, 2026

  • Workers and unionsSaid

    In July 2026, Colom said he would fight to raise the federal minimum wage, which has been $7.25 an hour since 2009.

    "I will fight to raise the minimum wage"

    Mississippi hasn't elected a Democrat to the U.S. Senate since 1982. Scott Colom believes he will change that in November, Magnolia Tribune, July 17, 2026

    Asked about global conflicts in July 2026, Colom said the focus should be on America and Mississippi, and that the U.S. should support its allies in a way that puts America first.

On the record

  • IsraelSaid

    In a June 2026 interview, Pinkins said he does not take support from pro-Israel lobbying groups, pointing to donations his Republican and Democratic opponents have received from people tied to those groups.

    "I'm the only candidate in this race that is not affiliated with pro-Israel lobbies like J Street and especially AIPAC"

    Independent U.S. Senate candidate Pinkins believes many Mississippi voters want something different, Magnolia Tribune, June 15, 2026

  • ICE

    No public statement found as of October 18, 2026.

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    On February 5, 2025, Pinkins's campaign posted a press release on his site headlined as criticism of the tariffs backed by President Trump and Sen. Hyde-Smith, saying they meant higher prices and fewer jobs.

    "Cindy Hyde-Smith and Donald Trump's Tariff Disaster: Higher Prices,Fewer Jobs, and Betrayed Mississippians"

    Cindy Hyde-Smith and Donald Trump's Tariff Disaster: Higher Prices,Fewer Jobs, and Betrayed Mississippians, Candidate's campaign site, February 5, 2025

  • Where they want the country to goSaid

    In a June 2026 interview, Pinkins said both the Republican and Democratic parties in Washington have failed, and that he is running as an independent who would not caucus with either party.

    "Both political parties have failed us. Where we are today proves that"

    Independent U.S. Senate candidate Pinkins believes many Mississippi voters want something different, Magnolia Tribune, June 15, 2026

  • Health careSaid

    Pinkins's campaign site says he supports the Medicare for All Act and calls health care a right.

    "I believe healthcare is a fundamental human right—not a privilege based on your job, your ZIP code, or your luck."

    Healthcare, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

  • Money in politicsSaid

    In June 2026, Pinkins said he does not take money from wealthy donors, lobbyists or super PACs, and that he has not sent fundraising texts or emails since January 2026.

    "I don't accept money from millionaires and billionaires, lobbyists or super PACs. I'm the only candidate in this race that does that."

    Independent U.S. Senate candidate Pinkins believes many Mississippi voters want something different, Magnolia Tribune, June 15, 2026

  • FarmingSaid

    Pinkins's campaign site says federal farm policy has favored large corporations and that he would push for farm bills written for small family farmers.

    "As your next U.S. Senator, I'll make sure that federal farm bills are written with small family farmers in mind."

    Jobs & Economy, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

I want my senator to refuse money from AIPAC PAC and United Democracy Project, and to say so publicly.

Call

    Tips for calling
    • Call your own senators and representative. Offices mostly count calls from their own voters.
    • Say your ZIP so the call counts as a constituent.
    • You'll talk to a staff member, not the official. That's normal: they log your call.
    • One or two issues per call is best.
    • Voicemail counts. Offices go through it every day.
    • If the D.C. office is busy, try a state office, or the U.S. Capitol Switchboard at (202) 224-3121.
    • Call again next week.
