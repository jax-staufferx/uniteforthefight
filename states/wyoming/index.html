James Byrd

Democratic 1% on Kalshi

Campaign website

On the record

  • Israel

    No public statement found as of October 18, 2026.

  • ICESaid

    Asked in a June 2026 WyoFile candidate questionnaire to rate the Trump administration's approach to immigration since 2025, Byrd said the border must be secured and the law enforced, but that policies separating families violate the nation's principles; he called for Congress to pass comprehensive reform and fund immigration courts.

    "While we must secure our borders and enforce the rule of law, policies that separate families and ignore human dignity violate our nation's core principles."

    James Byrd | U.S. Senate | WyoFile 2026 Election Guide, WyoFile, June 2026

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    In a September 2026 campaign statement after a 21-ton emergency food delivery to Cheyenne food pantries, Byrd said Wyoming's congressional delegation backs the administration's priorities while Wyoming families struggle with food costs.

    "Our own congressional delegation stands by and backs this administration’s failed priorities while Wyoming pays the price."

    21 TONS OF FOOD IS GONE IN 7 DAYS": JAMES BYRD BLASTS WASHINGTON FOR FUNDING FOREIGN WARS WHILE WYOMING GOES HUNGRY, Candidate's campaign site, September 17, 2026

  • Where they want the country to goSaid

    At a July 8, 2026 League of Women Voters candidate forum in Casper, Byrd said the shift of power from Congress to the president is not what the founders wanted, that Congress acquiesces to the executive, and that he would work to restore constitutional limits.

    "What I will do is go back there and put constitutional rules back into place."

    Casper forum separates US Senate candidates on healthcare, executive power, WyoFile, July 8, 2026

  • Foreign policySaid

    On October 7, 2026, Byrd criticized remarks President Trump made about the war with Iran at a rally in Grand Island, Nebraska; his campaign's statement quotes Trump as saying Iran could "take out" Los Angeles or San Diego and calling it "a very small price to pay."

    "No American city, no military base, and no American family is expendable"

    James Byrd Condemns Trump’s Rally Remarks on U.S. Cities and Degradative Political Rhetoric, Candidate's campaign site, October 7, 2026

  • Health careSaid

    In the June 2026 WyoFile questionnaire, Byrd said Congress should protect rural hospitals, make telehealth reimbursement permanent, and lower costs through price transparency and caps on prescription drug costs.

    "Congress can lower out-of-pocket expenses by pushing for transparent medical pricing, capping soaring prescription drug costs, and protecting consumer-friendly provisions in federal health insurance markets"

    James Byrd | U.S. Senate | WyoFile 2026 Election Guide, WyoFile, June 2026

  • EnergySaid

    In the June 2026 WyoFile questionnaire, Byrd said he would back federal permitting reform and programs modeled on the Wyoming Energy Dominance Fund to support new energy technology such as the TerraPower Natrium nuclear project.

    "As a federal lawmaker, I would champion federal permitting reform and scale up initiatives modeled after the state's newly established Wyoming Energy Dominance Fund"

    James Byrd | U.S. Senate | WyoFile 2026 Election Guide, WyoFile, June 2026

  • FarmingSaid

    In a May 2026 Cody Enterprise interview, Byrd said tariffs, the war in Iran and a deal to buy beef from Brazil have added to the economic stress on Wyoming farmers and ranchers, and that the federal government should put domestic producers first.

    "Quit screwing with the market so producers have a fair playing field"

    Byrd will champion working-class Wyoming, Cody Enterprise, May 27, 2026

Harriet Hageman

Republican 99% on Kalshi

U.S. Representative for Wyoming's at-large district · Campaign website

On the record

  • IsraelVoted

    On April 20, 2024, Hageman voted for H.R. 8034, the Israel Security Supplemental Appropriations Act, 2024, which provided emergency security funding for Israel. It passed the House 366-58.

    Roll call: Roll Call 152 | Bill Number: H. R. 8034, Office of the Clerk, U.S. House of Representatives, April 20, 2024

  • ICEVoted

    On June 9, 2026, Hageman voted for House passage of S. 2, the Secure America Act, the budget reconciliation bill that funds ICE and Customs and Border Protection. It passed 214-212.

    Roll call: Roll Call 214 | Bill Number: S. 2, Office of the Clerk, U.S. House of Representatives, June 9, 2026

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    In a statement after President Trump's State of the Union address on February 24, 2026, Hageman listed what she called the administration's steps on regulation, energy, the military and the southern border.

    "The Trump administration has already taken significant steps to cut burdensome regulations, boost American energy production, strengthen our military, and secure our southern border."

    Congresswoman Hageman Releases Statement Following President Trump's State of the Union Address, U.S. House of Representatives (office of Rep. Harriet Hageman), February 24, 2026

  • Where they want the country to goSaid

    In the same February 24, 2026 statement, Hageman said House Republicans are working to write the President's policies into federal law, and that she looks forward to continuing to work with him.

    "House Republicans are moving at the speed of Trump to codify the President’s successes into federal law."

    Congresswoman Hageman Releases Statement Following President Trump's State of the Union Address, U.S. House of Representatives (office of Rep. Harriet Hageman), February 24, 2026

  • TaxesVoted

    On July 3, 2025, Hageman voted to pass the final version of H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, the budget reconciliation bill of tax and spending changes. It passed the House 218-214.

    Roll call: Roll Call 190 | Bill Number: H. R. 1, Office of the Clerk, U.S. House of Representatives, July 3, 2025

  • EnergySaid

    On June 4, 2026, after President Trump announced a $700 million investment in coal plants and energy infrastructure, including a new West Coast export terminal, Hageman praised the move.

    "The world wants Wyoming’s clean coal to power their economies."

    Congresswoman Hageman Applauds Trump's Historic Action to Strengthen Domestic Coal Industry, U.S. House of Representatives (office of Rep. Harriet Hageman), June 4, 2026

  • Foreign policySaid

    In a March 2, 2026 statement on the U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran, Hageman backed President Trump's military action and said Iran's nuclear program had to be stopped.

    "Iran’s history of killing American troops, supporting terrorist networks, and refusal to cooperate in good-faith diplomacy made their nuclear-arms campaign a crusade that must be stopped."

    Congresswoman Hageman Releases Statement on Decisive Action Against Iran, U.S. House of Representatives (office of Rep. Harriet Hageman), March 2, 2026

  • Health careSaid

    Asked in a June 2026 WyoFile candidate questionnaire what Congress can do about healthcare costs and access, Hageman pointed to federal Rural Health Transformation Program money for Wyoming and said rural care should be considered in any national reform.

    "I am proud to have secured more than $205 million for Wyoming health care through the Rural Health Transformation Program to strengthen care and access across our state."

    Harriet Hageman | U.S. Senate | WyoFile 2026 Election Guide, WyoFile, June 2026

Your script

Hi, my name is ____, and I'm a constituent from ____, ZIP ____.

I'm calling about AIPAC money, ICE and checks and balances.

I want my senator to refuse money from AIPAC PAC and United Democracy Project, and to say so publicly.

I want my senator to vote against more money for ICE detention and enforcement.

I want my senator to use Congress's oversight power, including hearings, subpoenas and votes, whichever party is in charge.

Please record my position. Thank you for your time.

Some offices log one issue per call. If they do, call back for the rest.

Call

    Tips for calling
    • Call your own senators and representative. Offices mostly count calls from their own voters.
    • Say your ZIP so the call counts as a constituent.
    • You'll talk to a staff member, not the official. That's normal: they log your call.
    • One or two issues per call is best.
    • Voicemail counts. Offices go through it every day.
    • If the D.C. office is busy, try a state office, or the U.S. Capitol Switchboard at (202) 224-3121.
    • Call again next week.
