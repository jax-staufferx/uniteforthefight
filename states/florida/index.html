On the record

    "it is more important than ever to provide a buffer zone for these men and women enforcing the rule of law"

On the record

  • IsraelSaid

    In a June 29, 2026 statement on receiving the endorsement of Track AIPAC (Citizens Against AIPAC), a group opposing AIPAC's political spending, Nixon said she would reject attempts to "defend apartheid" and restated her opposition to what she called genocide in Palestine.

    "I will continue to reject any attempts to silence dissent, defend apartheid, and fuel endless wars."

    Senate Candidate Angie Nixon Earns Track AIPAC Endorsement, Candidate's campaign site, June 29, 2026

  • ICESaid

    In a July 13, 2026 statement after fatal shootings by immigration agents in Houston and Maine, Nixon called for ending funding for ICE and abolishing the agency. Her platform calls for rebuilding any immigration enforcement function under new civilian oversight.

    "We must abolish ICE."

    State Rep. Angie Nixon Condemns Deadly ICE Shootings; Reaffirms Call to Abolish ICE, Candidate's campaign site, July 13, 2026

  • The Trump administrationSaid

    In a Sept. 26, 2026 statement on reports that the Trump administration was removing more than 750,000 people from Affordable Care Act coverage under an anti-fraud initiative, Nixon called the move an attack on working-class families.

    "is another direct attack on working-class families who are just trying to survive."

    Rep. Angie Nixon Condemns Trump Administration Decision To Kick 750,000+ Americans Off Health Insurance, Candidate's campaign site, September 26, 2026

  • Where they want the country to goSaid

    In her Aug. 18, 2026 primary victory statement, Nixon described her opponent, Sen. Ashley Moody, as a rubber stamp for the "MAGA agenda" and said her goal was to win the seat and help Democrats "take back the U.S. Senate."

    "Now, we're taking this fight directly to a DeSantis-appointed rubber stamp for the MAGA agenda"

    "Floridians Have Voted for Change" Democratic Senate Nominee Angie Nixon Victory Statement, Candidate's campaign site, August 18, 2026

  • Foreign policySaid

    On July 10, 2026, after further U.S. strikes on Iran, Nixon said the United States should not be at war with Iran and that the money should go to domestic needs.

    "I have been very clear that the U.S. should not be at war in Iran."

    U.S. Senate Candidate Angie Nixon Responds to Additional U.S. Strikes on Iran; Trump Saying Ceasefire "Over", Candidate's campaign site, July 10, 2026

  • Health careSaid

    Nixon's campaign priorities page (as of October 2026) supports a single national Medicare for All program with no premiums, deductibles or copays.

    "Medicare for All that covers every person in America — free at the point of service, with no premiums, no deductibles, no copays, and no surprise bills."

    Priorities - Angie Nixon for U.S. Senate, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

  • ImmigrationSaid

    Nixon's campaign priorities page (as of October 2026) calls for a path to citizenship for Dreamers and undocumented residents and a pause on deportations while enforcement practices are audited.

    "a swift path to citizenship for Dreamers and undocumented residents, and a moratorium on deportations pending a full audit of enforcement practices."

    Priorities - Angie Nixon for U.S. Senate, Candidate's campaign site, undated page, read October 18, 2026

  • Money in politicsSaid

    In her Aug. 18, 2026 primary victory statement, Nixon said her campaign took no corporate PAC money.

    "We won this primary without taking a dime of corporate PAC money."

    "Floridians Have Voted for Change" Democratic Senate Nominee Angie Nixon Victory Statement, Candidate's campaign site, August 18, 2026

Call

    Tips for calling
    • Call your own senators and representative. Offices mostly count calls from their own voters.
    • Say your ZIP so the call counts as a constituent.
    • You'll talk to a staff member, not the official. That's normal: they log your call.
    • One or two issues per call is best.
    • Voicemail counts. Offices go through it every day.
    • If the D.C. office is busy, try a state office, or the U.S. Capitol Switchboard at (202) 224-3121.
    • Call again next week.
